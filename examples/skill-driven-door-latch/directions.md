## The inventor and the smaller key

Open on a bulky key silhouette, then follow Linus Yale Jr. from bank safe work to a compact flat key for general-use locks. End on the change in the object people carry. The National Inventors Hall of Fame identifies U.S. patent 31,278 and describes that career change. A cylinder story would require separate evidence and geometry. It cannot explain the asymmetric catch of the passage-door latch. [NIHF biography](https://www.invent.org/inductees/linus-yale-jr)

## From a separate gesture to automatic catching

Stage the same person twice with both hands full. First show a generic slide bolt requiring a second gesture, then an automatic spring latch that catches on closing. Use a paired reconstruction to expose the design tradeoff. Present it as a comparison of designs. The current sources do not establish a dated, linear genealogy from one device to the other. The Emtek guide documents the orientation of a current latch. [Emtek instructions](sources/emtek-cf2-installation.pdf)

## The door that lets you push and stops your pull

Two hands are occupied by a paper grocery bag. The occupant nudges the door shut, then tries pulling gently on the return. Cut to a simple two-direction test. The beveled face meets the strike as the door closes. The retaining face meets the opposite edge on the return. Normal handle operation retracts the latch, and the occupant walks through. Use a warm original apartment dramatization, a cool orthographic model of contact at the two opposing faces, and an uninterrupted final action that answers the opening. Ground bevel orientation in Emtek CF 2.0 page 2 step 4B. Label the model as a kinematic illustration and exclude the hidden linkage, forces and part dimensions.
